Divorce & Custody in Duval, Clay, Nassau & St. Johns County: Where to File and What to Expect

By Jennifer M. Erlinger, Esq. · Erlinger Family Law · Serving Duval, Clay, St. Johns and Nassau Counties

“You can live in one county, work in another and raise your kids in a third. When you’re facing a divorce or custody case in Northeast Florida, ‘where do I even file?’ is a real question.”

1. Four counties, two judicial circuits

Florida’s courts are organized into judicial circuits. Under Florida law (Fla. Stat. §26.25), the Fourth Judicial Circuit is made up of Clay, Duval and Nassau Counties. St. Johns County is part of the Seventh Judicial Circuit, along with Flagler, Putnam and Volusia Counties.

Why does that matter to you? Because a family law case in Jacksonville and one in St. Augustine can involve different clerks, different forms checklists, different local procedures and different judges. Knowing which court your case belongs in is the first step toward a smoother process.

  • Duval County: cases are filed with the Clerk of Courts in Jacksonville.
  • Clay County: the county courthouse is in Green Cove Springs.
  • Nassau County: the county courthouse is in Fernandina Beach.
  • St. Johns County: the county courthouse is in St. Augustine.
“The courthouse is only one part of your case. The real question is: do you understand what happens next?”

2. Can you file at all? The six-month rule

To file for divorce in Florida, at least one spouse must have lived in the state for six continuous months immediately before filing (Fla. Stat. §61.021). Florida is also a no-fault state: you state that the marriage is “irretrievably broken” (Fla. Stat. §61.052) and do not need to prove anyone did anything wrong.

If you recently moved to Duval, Clay, Nassau or St. Johns County, the six months count toward Florida residency. You can ask an attorney where your particular case should be filed, especially if you and your spouse now live in different counties.

3. If you have children: the parenting plan and the parenting course

When a divorce involves minor children, Florida requires a parenting plan that sets out time-sharing and how major decisions will be made. Courts use standard forms, including the Parenting Plan (Form 12.995(a)).

Parents also must complete a parenting course. Under Fla. Stat. §61.21, all parties to a dissolution of marriage with minor children must complete a court-approved parent education course before the final judgment. The petitioner generally must complete it within 45 days after filing, and the other spouse within 45 days after being served. A parent who doesn’t attend can face consequences, including contempt of court or limits on time-sharing.

The Nassau County Clerk’s checklist, for example, lists several approved course providers, including online options. Check with your county’s clerk for the current list before you register.

4. The paperwork: one missing form can slow everything down

Every clerk’s office wants the case started correctly. For example, Nassau County’s checklist for a dissolution of marriage with minor children lists these items among those needed to start a case:

  • Petition for Dissolution of Marriage with Dependent or Minor Child(ren)
  • Family Law Financial Affidavit
  • UCCJEA Affidavit (information about where the children have lived)
  • Parenting Plan
  • Notice of Related Cases and Notice of Social Security Number
  • Proof of Florida residency, such as a driver’s license or voter registration
  • A civil cover sheet and the filing fee

After filing, your spouse must be formally served, and both spouses generally must exchange financial information (Fla. Fam. L. R. P. 12.285). Requirements and local practice can differ by county, so always confirm with your county clerk or an attorney.

5. Common mistakes we see before filing

  • Filing in a hurry without understanding residency, venue or the forms.
  • Skipping the parenting course or missing the 45-day window.
  • Moving out or changing the schedule without a plan for the children.
  • Posting about it on social media. It is not private in a courtroom.
  • Waiting too long to ask questions. Early advice often saves time and stress.

Frequently asked questions

Which county do I file in if my spouse and I live in different counties?

It depends on the facts. An attorney can look at where each of you lives and where your children live to advise you on the right place to file.

Is Nassau County in the same judicial circuit as Duval County?

Yes. Duval, Clay and Nassau Counties are all in the Fourth Judicial Circuit. St. Johns County is in the Seventh Judicial Circuit.

Do I need to take a parenting class to get divorced in Florida?

If you have minor children, yes. Parties to a dissolution of marriage with minor children generally must complete a court-approved parenting course before the final judgment.

How long do I have to live in Florida before filing for divorce?

At least one spouse must have lived in Florida for six continuous months before filing.
